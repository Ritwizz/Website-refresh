The art of concealing

A line of home appliances that complement luxury apartments in India.

Industrial / Product Design Home appliances 2021–22
It's hard to sell luxury homes with poorly designed, run of the mill appliances populating them.
We hid the inverter in plain sight by giving it a new persona; the UPS, by putting it centre-stage.
The bathroom heater changes persona entirely — from utilitarian appliance to contemporary art.
The play dustbin conceals itself by vaguely resembling a cartoon character, bright accents and all.
Six appliances, taken from strategy through to working prototypes on the workshop floor.

From January to July 2021, I worked as a design intern at Core Experience, where I led the design and development of six unique home appliances for Svasa Homes' luxury residences. The project aimed to elevate the interior experience of high-end apartments through thoughtfully integrated product design. Acting as the lead designer, I worked primarily independently, while regularly collaborating with a multidisciplinary team of designers, architects and engineers for feedback and alignment.

A lot of work went into figuring out which household appliances cause the biggest disturbances and what the consequences of changing their user experience and aesthetics might look like. This laid a fantastic groundwork and had a massive impact on the process going forward. We also mapped the typical home interior and the market to find intervention points and choose which products to take forward.

We hid the inverter, otherwise an ugly home appliance, in plain sight by giving it a new persona. It doubles as an entrance console, a piece of furniture designed to house all your day-to-day essentials. It stands near your home entrance to give you one simple solution to store your everyday carry and keep your secondary devices charged. The UPS took the opposite approach: it is hidden by being put centre-stage, adorning a user-customizable LED matrix screen on the front so it reads as an accessory rather than a utility.

We hid the bathroom water heater by concealing it as artwork, completely changing its persona from boring utilitarian appliance to contemporary modern art. The kitchen water heater conceals itself behind a gently curved panel of plastic, aiming to disappear in plain sight by attracting the least possible attention to the fact that it's a water heater.

The play dustbin is aimed at environments primarily meant for kids, so it conceals itself by vaguely resembling a cartoon character with bright coloured accents, blending into any colourful environment it's placed in. The sphere dustbin is aimed at living spaces where the overall aesthetic matters, disguising what it is by taking an unconventional form that reads more like a piece of technology or furniture.

The line was taken past renders: prototypes were built in the workshop — perforated panels, metal enclosures, plywood structures and shells — to verify that the concealment held up at full scale, in the hand, and in the room.
