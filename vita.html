The grand touring philosophy applied to a scooter for people in their early 20s.

Full account

In early 2023, I owned an Aprilia SR 150. I chose excitement over practicality and always wondered why both couldn't co-exist. When I asked around, I realized many people felt the same. That was the motivation behind this project.

Vita takes the grand touring philosophy — long distance capability without giving up the character of the machine — and applies it to a scooter aimed at people in their early 20s: students and first-job riders who need to carry things, ride every day, and still want something they enjoy looking at.

The process ran as a lot of sketching and Vizcom iterations, followed by refinement sketches and full development in Blender. Detail areas — the taillight, the rear, the headlight and the apron — were resolved as their own studies before the surfaces were locked.

Vita carries two large bags — one behind the rider, one on the footboard — and both attach and come off without tools. That constraint shaped the rear end: the scooter has to look finished with the bags on for a trip and equally deliberate with them off for the commute, rather than a machine missing a part.
